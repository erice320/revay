04
Primary ICP and no-go rules

Choose one H2 growth bet and make other segments explicitly secondary.

Use the revenue-complexity wedge first; use full PSA replacement only where workflow and migration fit are certified.

Primary

  • Established hybrid MSP/CSP/security or field-service providers.
  • Complex recurring, usage, device, project, billing, payment, and service workflows.
  • Launch MRR at or above $1,000 with credible 12-month expansion path.
  • Known buyer, implementation owner, data owner, supported integrations, and transition event.

Secondary

  • Tigerpaw installed-base accounts with validated migration readiness.
  • Billing-led or field-service/security workflow patterns where Rev.io has proof.
  • Accounts where revenue operations value is clear even if full replacement is not required.

Controlled replacement

  • General MSPs with acute PSA pain only after six-gate certification.
  • Replacement claims require customer signoff on gaps, workarounds, owners, and dates.

Stop / defer

  • Low-complexity MSPs seeking low-cost PSA/RMM bundle.
  • Unsupported custom workflows or no data owner.
  • No attach path, no transition event, or implementation economics that do not work.

06
Offer, pricing, expansion

A minimum MRR rule is not enough; H2 needs a productized offer architecture.

Package complexity should be visible before close instead of absorbed silently by implementation.

Offer architecture to approve

  • Entry offer for revenue-operations wedge.
  • Core billing/service operations package.
  • Payments attach model with verified economics.
  • Revii/AI offer only where workflow proof exists.
  • Implementation and migration tiers: Standard, Advanced, Complex.

Pricing principles

  • Keep $1,000 launch MRR floor as a screen, not the full ICP.
  • Add expected 12-month expansion and cost-to-serve to qualification.
  • Reconsider unsupported no-consultants/no-implementation-fee claims for complex accounts.
  • Define discount authority and expansion triggers.

Payments case required

  • Eligible installed-base accounts
  • Attach rate and payment volume
  • Net take rate and gross margin
  • Implementation effort and support burden
  • Verified fee/workflow comparison before using savings claims

AI monetization status

  • Treat AI billing as design-partner motion until referenceable customers exist.
  • Do not make AI token billing the core H2 revenue answer without demand, metering, product, pricing, and margin proof.

H2 demand and channel plan

Approve only the three highest-return motions and make channel strategy explicit.

Marketing tactics should be funded by expected MRR contribution, proof asset readiness, and kill thresholds.

Motion 1 - Proof-first demand capture

  • Audience: primary revenue-complexity ICP.
  • Offer: workflow-fit assessment and revenue-operations diagnostic.
  • Proof asset: complex billing/payment implementation proof.
  • Kill threshold: qualified opp and MRR conversion by source.

Motion 2 - Tigerpaw migration economics

  • Audience: validated Tigerpaw installed base.
  • Offer: migration readiness review.
  • Proof asset: migration case and timeline.
  • Kill threshold: migration capacity, close-to-live, and expansion MRR.

Motion 3 - Payments expansion

  • Audience: customers billing through Rev.io but collecting elsewhere.
  • Offer: reconciliation and payment economics review.
  • Proof asset: verified workflow and fee comparison.
  • Kill threshold: attach rate, payment volume, and gross margin.

Route-to-market decision

  • Choose H2 ecosystem strategy: technology-alliance-led, marketplace/distribution-led, referral/co-sell-led, or direct-led with ecosystem support.
  • Do not call integrations a channel strategy until partner economics, enablement, targets, and conflict rules are defined.
