PSA 2.0 roadmap review

Did the roadmap help us win the PSA customers we want?

Apollo 3 and Viking 1 addressed real product gaps. The board question is whether they help Rev.io win larger, better-fit PSA customers.

Roadmap logic

Built from real loss reasons

Lost deals repeatedly cited project management, integrations, quoting, mobile field work, reporting, portals, and agreements.

Strategy shift

The buyer bar is higher

The $1,000 floor makes casual, small-fit buyers less relevant. We now need operators with clearer pain, scale, and commitment.

Management focus

Turn product into pipeline

Package the releases, qualify harder, prove implementation readiness, and measure pipeline from the right accounts.

Acquisition impact
06 / 11

The release created activity. It did not create a recovery motion.

Sales had a reason to re-engage. The gap was turning that interest into owned PSA pipeline with buyer-specific next steps.

What happened

  • 92 ICP accounts had post-launch activity.
  • Only 13 reopened into PSA opportunities.
  • The largest group had meaningful engagement but no controlled opportunity path.

Why results did not follow

  • Friction shifted to timing, implementation risk, budget, contracts, and decision ownership.
  • Residual workflow and integration fit still mattered in several conversations.
  • The next play is not more activity — it is accountable recovery pipeline.
Onboarding and retention
07 / 11

Onboarding and retention start before the deal closes.

Do not claim retention impact yet. Use the roadmap review to decide which buyers Rev.io can confidently implement, support, and grow.

Product

Confirm what is truly solved, what is caveated, and what still needs depth.

Onboarding

Validate migration, integration, data, training, and change-management requirements.

Retention

Track adoption, support friction, expansion posture, and referenceability.

If a buyer is not likely to implement well, it is not a quality acquisition target.
