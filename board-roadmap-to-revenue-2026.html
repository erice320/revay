What shipped
05 / 10

Meaningful blockers moved—but “delivered” is internally inconsistent.

Integrations

QBD, NinjaOne, N-able, HubSpot, Portal.io, Datto and ConnectWise RMM records now show delivered/available states.

Core workflows

Project, agreements, quoting, mobile/GPS, ticketing, dispatch, reporting and knowledgebase capabilities show substantial delivery.

Measurement defect

Some records pair “Delivered” with In Dev, Dev Complete, Staged, In Progress or Returned to Product. Commercial readiness cannot be inferred from Delivery alone.

First conclusion: we need a buyer-readiness gate, not another delivery label.

Why momentum lagged
06 / 10

Five hypotheses—being tested account by account

1. Partial blocker clearance

Many losses contained multiple gaps. Shipping one integration does not make a yellow/red account re-engageable.

2. Parity ≠ presence

A feature can exist without meeting buyer depth, reliability or workflow expectations.

3. No activation motion

No universal named cohort, enablement SLA, launch owner, campaign, or weekly conversion review tied to each release.

4. Expectation debt

Cancellation evidence repeatedly connects product gaps with sales expectation mismatch and support/escalation friction.

5. Adoption friction

Migration, training, client bandwidth and implementation design can prevent a shipped capability from creating value.

Attribution gap

Roadmap, Salesforce, onboarding and usage are not joined into one release-to-MRR ledger.

09 / 10

Every priority release gets a revenue activation brief

Who

ICP, named accounts, prior losses, at-risk clients, expansion cohort.

Why now

Problem, competitive proof, buyer outcome, evidence and limitations.

How

Message, channel, demo, offer, sales play, onboarding readiness.

Accountability

Owner, launch date, weekly KPIs, gaps, decisions and help needed.

If a release has no cohort, proof, owner, launch and KPI, it is product output—not a revenue plan.

Board decision
10 / 10
The operating change

Install one weekly
Roadmap-to-Revenue review.

Decide

What is buyer-ready, what is not, which cohorts to activate, what to qualify out, and what needs executive help.

Measure

Shipment → proof → activation → adoption → pipeline → graduated and retained MRR.

The goal is not to prove the roadmap worked. It is to build a system that tells us quickly where it did, where it did not, and what action changes the outcome.
