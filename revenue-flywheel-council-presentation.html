Executive Thesis

Rev.io's problem is not that we cannot sell. It is that too much of what we sell does not yet become revenue we keep.

We are creating pipeline and closing deals, but bookings are running below plan, and the strongest measured leak is after the win: PSA implementations stall or cancel before they become live, durable customers.

Demand And GTM

We create pipeline, but lose too much before value is proven.

Data reference

Marketing evidence: 683 marketing-sourced leads, 423 marketing-created opportunities, 62 Closed Won totaling $44,128, and 350 Closed Lost totaling $322,421.90. Channel evidence: 467 channel/partner events and 30 CommerceHub-related closed-won opportunities.

Lifecycle signal

Closed Lost clustered before or at demo: 156 Discovery Scheduled, 86 Discovery Completed, and 71 Initial Product Demo. Loss patterns include timing, no-shows, missing capabilities, lack of decision-maker, no business issue, price, and non-response.

Action

Build a weekly source/product/ICP bookings bridge to shift from more pipeline to more qualified, provable, implementable pipeline.

What this proves

Rev.io is generating demand and converting some of it into opportunities and wins. Early-stage loss volume is meaningful enough to manage qualification, buyer readiness, product proof, and fit as one system.

What this does not prove

It does not prove Marketing is the root cause. It does not prove source ROI, CAC, payback, or profitable retained revenue by channel because source spend, onboarding, support, retention, and margin are not yet joined.

The Win

Closed Won is progress, not money in the bank.

Data reference

PSA evidence: 173 Closed Won PSA/PSA 2.0 opportunities totaling $119,044 MRR for opportunities closing since 2025-07-01. Recent marketing evidence showed PSA 2.0 at $6,300 of $6,461 May closed-won marketing MRR.

Lifecycle signal

Finance evidence showed April closed-won bookings of $20,059 MRR versus $60,074.75 budget MRR, or 33.4% attainment, and May-to-date $10,850 versus $62,725.50, or 17.3% in the prior run.

Action

Report wins by product motion and lifecycle status. Require RevOps/Finance confirmation of Amount/MRR field semantics before using totals as final board financial facts.

What this proves

There is real PSA/PSA 2.0 win activity, but total booked MRR is under plan. Product motion matters: PSA 2.0 shows recent marketing-sourced win strength while other motions need diagnosis.

What this does not prove

It does not prove PSA 2.0 is the best profitable growth engine until onboarding, support, retention, and margin are joined. It does not prove Billing or Cyber Protect are weak products; it proves recent conversion underperformed forecast.

Onboarding Value Realization

The strongest measured leak is after the win.

Data reference

Close-to-kickoff is not the main measured problem: median 7 days, p75 13. Kickoff-to-actual graduation is the concern: median 79 days, p75 134, with only 35 actual graduation records.

Lifecycle signal

Onboarding evidence found 98 active records lacking graduation, 29 return-to-sales/on-hold, 36 with no milestones, and 7 kickoff-before-close data issues. Green River data showed 81 canceled, 18 return-to-sales, 13 at risk, and 8 on hold. PSA Web cancellations represented $37,379 booked MRR / $448,548 ARR.

Action

Launch a PSA Rescue and Prevention Board with owner, blocker, exposure, save/close/qualify-out decision, next action, due date, and escalation.

What this proves

The strongest measured lifecycle leak is not getting customers started; it is converting started/won PSA customers into quality go-live and durable revenue. Canceled/stalled PSA Web exposure is a board-level growth issue.

What this does not prove

It does not yet quantify margin impact, churn causality, or support-cost drag by blocker type. Automated blocker categories still need human review before becoming leadership metrics.

Support, Adoption, And Trust

Complexity lands in Support when it is not prevented upstream.

Data reference

Usage evidence: 125 accounts, 152,468 events, 82 strategic adopters, and 22 newly tracked accounts. Support evidence: Billing created since 2026-02-28 = 2,243, unsolved = 201, last 30 days = 701; PSA created = 1,056, unsolved = 99, last 30 days = 270.

Lifecycle signal

Trust readiness is a separate sales-confidence gate. SOC 2 evidence showed 208 controls: 177 Not Verified, 20 Operating, 9 Operating Effectively, 1 Operating Needs Improvement, and 1 Evidence Collected.

Action

Create a Revenue Friction Watchlist and deal-ready verified trust packet. Define reference readiness using usage, support health, onboarding quality, renewal posture, account owner validation, and customer outcome.

What this proves

Support pressure is material enough to include in the revenue lifecycle, especially for Billing and PSA. Trust readiness can become sales diligence friction. Usage signals can help identify strategic adopters and at-risk tracked accounts.

What this does not prove

It does not prove support tickets caused churn, margin leakage, or expansion loss. It does not prove usage equals reference readiness, or that SOC 2 accelerates sales until deal-critical controls and response SLAs are verified.

Product As The Lifecycle Multiplier

Product activity exists. The revenue linkage does not.

Data reference

Roadmap evidence: 704 records, including 262 Delivered, 67 On Track, and 20 At Risk. Pod dashboard evidence: 490 total features, 278 available, 42 in progress, 170 not started, 20 at risk, and 77 on track.

Lifecycle signal

Visible roadmap relations to customer, onboarding, and feedback links returned zero linked records. Missing-capability themes appear in PSA/PSA 2.0 loss evidence: project management, mobile/GPS, job costing, progressive billing, integrations, and sales opportunities.

Action

Build a Product Revenue Evidence Ledger for the top 25 PSA/value-realization blockers. Classify each blocker as sell-around, qualify-out, roadmap-commit, workaround, enablement fix, price/scope change, or stop-selling-until-fixed.

What this proves

Product delivery is active, but roadmap activity is not yet tied to customer lifecycle outcomes. Rev.io has enough product, loss, onboarding, and support evidence to build a revenue-prioritized blocker ledger.

What this does not prove

It does not prove shipping more features alone will improve growth. It does not prove which roadmap items create the most retained revenue until blockers are joined to opportunity, go-live, support, retention, and margin.

30-Day Action Plan Overview

The first 30 days should create an operating cadence, not a research project.

Weekly meeting

Owner: Evan.
Participants: Finance, RevOps, Sales, Marketing, Channel, Product, Onboarding, CS/Support, and Security.

Weekly output

What to scale, what to fix, what to qualify out, what to stop, and which data defect has an owner/SLA.

Product Revenue Evidence Ledger

Product + Product Ops.

Source/Product/ICP Bookings Bridge

Sales + Marketing + Channel + RevOps.

PSA Rescue and Prevention Board

Onboarding + Product + CS/Support.

Revenue Friction + Trust Watchlist

CS/Support + Product + Security.

Workstream 1

Product Revenue Evidence Ledger.

Build in first 30 days
  • Start with top 25 PSA/value-realization blockers.
  • Capture missing capability/workflow, related losses, roadmap item, onboarding blocker, support theme, ARR/MRR exposure if available, owner, and next decision.
  • Classify each blocker as sell-around, qualify-out, roadmap-commit, workaround, enablement fix, price/scope change, or stop-selling-until-fixed.
Decisions enabled
  • Which product promises Sales can safely make now.
  • Which blockers need roadmap commitment.
  • Which gaps should be sold around or qualified out.
  • Which roadmap work should be deprioritized because it is not tied to revenue friction.
Workstream 2

Source/Product/ICP Bookings Bridge.

Build in first 30 days
  • Bridge source/campaign/partner, product motion, ICP/segment, stage, owner, loss reason, proof gap, decision-maker status, missing-feature signal, Closed Won amount/MRR field basis, and next action.
  • Separate PSA 2.0, Billing, Payments, Cyber Protect, Channel/CommerceHub, add-ons, and other motions.
  • Treat missing source, product motion, ICP, or loss reason as named defects with owner and SLA.
Decisions enabled
  • Which sources and motions deserve more effort.
  • Which early losses are fixable through proof, qualification, or decision-maker access.
  • Which demand should be rejected before it creates onboarding/support drag.
Workstream 3

PSA Rescue And Prevention Board.

Build in first 30 days
  • Start with canceled, return-to-sales, on-hold, not-kicked-off, aged, off-track, and missing-milestone PSA accounts.
  • Each row needs account, product motion, source/rep if available, booked MRR/ARR if available, current state, blocker, blocker class, owner, decision, next action, due date, and escalation path.
  • Add a pre-kickoff readiness gate for migration, integrations, billing/payment complexity, unsupported workflow acceptance, client bandwidth, and product-blocker disposition.
Decisions enabled
  • Which accounts can be saved.
  • Which should be closed out or reset.
  • Which blockers should change sales qualification.
  • Which blockers need product or support escalation.
Workstream 4

Revenue Friction + Trust Watchlist.

Build in first 30 days
  • Create a watchlist for top Billing and PSA support drivers, repeated accounts, unsolved/aged/high/urgent signals, onboarding stage, account owner, product owner, MRR/ARR where available, renewal/reference status, and next action.
  • Add usage/adoption signals for strategic adopters and at-risk tracked accounts, labeled as usage signals, not retention proof.
  • Create a deal-ready trust packet for verified SOC 2/security claims with owner SLA for sales diligence responses.
Decisions enabled
  • Which support drivers need product fixes, enablement, workflow changes, or qualification changes.
  • Which accounts are not reference-ready.
  • Which trust gaps may block enterprise sales conversations.
30-Day Cadence And Deliverables

The month ends with decisions, not a longer question list.

Week 1

Confirm owners, cadence, source systems, and first data fields. Select first 25 PSA/value-realization blockers and first at-risk PSA account list.

Week 2

Review first Product Evidence Ledger, Bookings Bridge, PSA Rescue Board, and Friction Watchlist. Assign owner/SLA for missing fields.

Week 3

Make first operating decisions: qualify-out rules, sell-around guidance, rescue actions, support/product escalations, and trust-response owners.

Week 4

Present first lifecycle scorecard: what moved, what was fixed, what was stopped, what still lacks evidence, and which decisions need Evan/board escalation.

By Day 90

What leaders should be able to decide.

  • Which product promises we can confidently sell.
  • Which sources and product motions create qualified, implementable opportunities.
  • Which deals should be qualified out before they hurt onboarding and support.
  • Which implementation blockers need product, enablement, pricing, or process intervention.
  • Which customers are healthy enough to become references.
  • Which motions are creating retained, profitable revenue or the best available cost-to-serve proxy.
This does not require perfect data. It requires a weekly operating cadence where gaps have owners and actions.
Data Integrity

What not to overclaim.

  • CAC or payback.
  • Source ROI.
  • Customer-level gross margin.
  • Retention, churn, or expansion causality.
  • Product feature count as revenue impact.
  • Support ticket count as revenue impact.
  • Salesforce Amount/MRR totals without field-definition confirmation.

The recommendation does not depend on those claims. It depends on the visible lifecycle pattern and the need to manage it directly.

Board Ask

Approve a 90-day Product-to-GTM-to-Onboarding-to-Support lifecycle program.

The program should be owned by Evan and operated weekly with Finance, RevOps, Sales, Marketing, Channel, Product, Onboarding, CS/Support, and Security.

The board is not being asked to approve a premature budget answer. It is being asked to approve the operating system that will show what to scale, fix, narrow, reprice, qualify out, or stop.
Closing

Scale what can be sold, implemented, supported, referenced, and retained profitably.

The growth engine has raw material: demand, product activity, wins, onboarding starts, usage, support signals, and customer proof opportunities.

The work now is to convert that raw material into a disciplined lifecycle. Everything that does not convert should be fixed, narrowed, repriced, qualified out, or stopped.
