PSA 2.0 roadmap review

Why roadmap deliveries have not converted into the expected MRR unlock

This deck answers the operating question from ER and RK: what we expected the PSA roadmap to unlock, what actually happened, and what still has to change across GTM, onboarding, and product execution.

Roadmap logic

Built on real loss reasons

Lost deals repeatedly cited project management, integrations, quoting, mobile field work, reporting, portals, and agreements.

Strategy shift

A higher buyer bar

The $1,000 floor makes casual, small-fit buyers less relevant. We now need operators with clearer pain, scale, and commitment.

Management focus

Turn product into pipeline

Package the releases, qualify harder, prove implementation readiness, and measure pipeline from the right accounts.

Why didn’t re-engagement create the sales momentum we expected?

Ryan and Robin’s broader reopened-opportunity view confirms the same pattern: lost PSA buyers will re-enter the conversation, but most do not yet convert into durable recovery pipeline.

ICP post-launch lens

13 reopened opps

Only 14% of 92 active ICP accounts reopened into PSA pipeline; 5 remain active and 8 re-lost.

Recovery wins

0 of 103

No ICP-size recovery wins yet in the post-launch cohort we tested against the roadmap.

What the second losses tell us

  • Some gaps remain: SWMC is still the largest second-loss reason in the broader reopened set.
  • The friction shifted: discovery no-shows, timing, decision-maker involvement, price, contracts, and competitors are now material loss paths.
  • The buyer bar is higher: re-engaged accounts need confidence in implementation, workflow change, integrations, and economic value.

What this means for the board

  • Product progress created access: the roadmap gave Sales permission to reopen real conversations.
  • Access is not a motion: too many accounts were touched or reopened without a controlled buyer-owned change event.
  • Pipeline discipline is the gap: recovery requires a named owner, a dated plan, and an opportunity created when the discussion becomes real.
Board takeaway: the roadmap reopened the door. The next management task is turning reactivation into a repeatable recovery motion — with qualification, implementation readiness, and commercial accountability built in.
Question 5
07 / 11

What is still ailing GTM, onboarding, and product execution?

The issue is not a single broken handoff. The remaining gap sits across buyer qualification, implementation confidence, product proof, and the operating discipline to turn interest into retained revenue.

GTM

Qualification, packaging, discovery, and opportunity creation are not yet disciplined enough to convert re-engagement into pipeline.

Onboarding

Buyers still need confidence that Rev.io can handle migration, integrations, data readiness, training, and change management.

Product execution

The roadmap closed real gaps, but buyers still ask for proof depth, workflow parity, and credible timing on remaining dependencies.

Management read: the next unlock is not another release announcement. It is a tighter operating system from roadmap promise to qualified pipeline to successful implementation.

3

4

Track the result

Opportunity, win, loss, go-live, retention signal.
