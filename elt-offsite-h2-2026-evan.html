ICP And Value Planning

ICP Is A Fit System, Not A Persona

Prioritize operators with scale, attach potential, operational volume, and implementation readiness.

Fit Screen

  • $3M-$100M revenue or clear operating scale; employee count is context, not the gate.
  • $1,000+ launch MRR with credible path to $2,000+ in 12 months.
  • Enough invoice, payment, ticket, service, usage, or dispatch volume to create retained value.
  • Named buyer, implementation owner, data cleanup owner, source systems, required integrations, and transition event.

Lanes And Gate

  • Three lanes: Billing-led CSP, security / field-service PSA, and MSP / IT services workflow pain.
  • Expansion path: Payments, PSA, usage, automation, Revii, and add-ons.
  • Revii fit requires clean customer, ticket, billing, payment, workflow, integration, and permission data.
  • Walk away under $1,000 launch MRR when there is no attach path, urgent pain, owner, buyer, data readiness, or supported workflow.

Why The Cancelled PSA Accounts Matter

The $800+ MRR cancelled cohort shows retention risk forming before go-live, when product-fit gaps, data complexity, and unclear expectations collide during onboarding.

What We Learned

  • These were not all bad-fit or low-value accounts; many were operationally complex MSP, security, and service businesses Rev.io should want to win.
  • The largest failure pattern was value-realization friction: implementation exposed product gaps, data limits, reporting gaps, and workarounds before customers felt live value.
  • Sales expectation mismatch appeared in clear cases: POS, Newport, and Global Data expected capabilities or timelines that onboarding could not make real quickly enough.
  • Data and integration complexity showed up as a retention risk across Global Data, Newport, HELP, CPT, and similar accounts.

What Must Change

  • Qualify implementation fit before close.
  • Create a risk handoff from Sales to Onboarding.
  • Define product-dependent onboarding gates.
  • Treat onboarding as retention creation, not project administration.
  • Close the evidence loop back into ICP, sales talk tracks, product, and implementation playbooks.

Retention / Creating Fans

Feature-Level Pattern 1: Workflow Replacement Broke In Specific Places

The cancellations were not just “platform concerns.” Customers hit concrete blockers in the workflows they expected Rev.io PSA to replace on day one.

Quoting, CPQ, Sales Workflow

  • Hide/remove pricing on quotes was not available or incomplete.
  • Quote recipients could not adjust quantities or make quote changes after send.
  • Sales rep tracking and sales funnel visibility were manual or unclear.
  • Quote-to-ticket and quote-to-invoice flow did not feel operationally complete.
  • External proposal tools like Quote Valet and HubSpot workflows were not cleanly replaced.
Seen in: POS Innovation, NetWatch, CMJ/Securco, HELP, Obera, Security Vision.

Billing, Packages, Invoice Logic

  • Package quantity and child-product masking did not support required quote-to-invoice behavior.
  • Parent-child billing rollups and multi-location billing blocked operational workflows.
  • Proration, pricing automation, invoice behavior, tax mapping, and GL handling needed workarounds.
  • Customers needed sales/use-tax and QuickBooks reconciliation reports they could trust.
  • Comparative billing expectations were not consistently defined before implementation.
Seen in: Global Data, Newport, ITR, CPT, Losh.

Time, Payroll, Utilization

  • Hourly employees needed time entry outside ticket work.
  • Clock-in/out vs. ticket time separation was confusing for customers.
  • Billable and non-billable code classification affected billing and payroll confidence.
  • Time rounding and utilization reporting were viewed as inaccurate or hard to operationalize.
  • Customers needed payroll-ready exports, not just activity capture.
Seen in: Hiscall, Newport.
Readout: These are workflow replacement failures. The buyer expected PSA to become the daily operating system, not a partially configured tool that required parallel spreadsheets, legacy systems, or manual reconciliation.

Retention / Creating Fans

Feature-Level Pattern 2: Migration, Field Ops, And Integrations Made Go-Live Feel Unsafe

The second cluster was not one feature. It was the customer asking, “Can I run my company from this system without losing history, field execution, or integration continuity?”

Project Management, Dispatch, Field Ops

  • Labor forecasting, live dashboards, capacity planning, and project management depth were not where customers expected.
  • Dispatch, scheduling, and ticket workflows required extra training and still felt incomplete for some operators.
  • Mobile readiness and field usage concerns appeared in at-risk accounts.
  • GPS expectation mismatch surfaced: mobile-device tracking vs. vehicle-based tracking in low-signal environments.
  • Customers needed the field workflow to be proven before go-live, not discovered during training.
Seen in: NetWatch, CMJ/Securco, ITR, Newport.

Data Migration And History

  • Customers wanted to start with real operating data, not a demo database.
  • Product lists, customer lists, legacy tickets, documents, customer numbers, and historical records became go-live blockers.
  • Limited historical migration created fear of losing searchable operating history.
  • Incomplete or failed data pulls slowed implementation trust.
  • Quote training and template setup could not be meaningful without accurate product/customer data.
Seen in: POS Innovation, Security Vision, HELP, CPT, ITR.

External System Fit

  • QuickBooks/QBO, HubSpot, Quote Valet, monitoring systems, package/order APIs, and custom data flows mattered to daily operations.
  • Customers needed Rev.io to become the system of record, not another tool beside legacy systems.
  • Warranty lookup, MAC-address/device lookup, support-to-sales handoff, and monitoring-to-ticket flow showed up as operational needs.
  • Integration requirements were often discovered in onboarding instead of qualified before close.
  • When integration fit was unclear, customers treated go-live as unsafe.
Seen in: Newport, Obera, HELP, Loricca, Global Data.
Offsite implication: Add a pre-close PSA workflow-fit check for quoting, billing/package model, payroll/time, field/mobile, data migration, and required integrations. If any item is product-dependent, it needs an owner, date, and accepted workaround before the deal is treated as implementation-ready.
