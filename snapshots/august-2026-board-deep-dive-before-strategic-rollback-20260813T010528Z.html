August 2026 Board Deep Dive

We raised the bar on the buyer.
Now the roadmap has to clear that bar.

The move from a $500 to $1,000 minimum was not just a pricing change. It was a choice to sell to buyers with stronger fit, larger stakes, and more commitment. Apollo 3 and Viking 1 now need to be judged through that lens.

Board preparation • August 2026 • Salesforce + roadmap refreshed Aug 11
Executive answer
02 / 11

We do not believe this was the wrong roadmap.

What we believe

The roadmap addressed real buyer needs: integrations, project work, field workflows, quoting, reporting, portals, agreements, and accounting.

What changed

Our commercial standard moved up. We are no longer optimizing for a buyer casually testing PSA at $500.

What we have to prove

That the features we built matter to the higher-conviction, higher-value buyer we now want.

What we do next

Run the roadmap back through product diligence, packaging, positioning, and account-level plays for the updated ICP.

Strategic context
03 / 11

The minimum change was a strategy choice.

The $1,000 floor was designed to improve buyer quality, not simply lift price. It was meant to pull Rev.io toward clients with enough complexity, urgency, and commitment to realize the value of a premium PSA platform.

$500
Old anchor

Allowed too many low-commitment buyers to casually try the product.

$1,000
New floor

Requires better diligence and stronger conviction before the sale.

5–6x
Target deal ambition

The strategic target is meaningfully larger than what Sales had often been selling.

That means our product story and sales plays have to be rebuilt for the buyer we want.

What the data says
04 / 11

The roadmap matched real pain, but the buyer bar moved.

Real demand existed

Historic losses repeatedly cited integrations, project management, mobile and field workflows, CPQ, reporting, portals, agreements, and accounting.

Product work was directionally aligned

Apollo 3 and Viking 1 shipped into several of those pain areas. This was not random roadmap work.

The open question is sharper now

Did we build enough of the right workflow, at the right depth, for the larger and more committed buyer we are now pursuing?

That question needs diligence

Not a slogan. Not a release note. A structured review of what shipped, who asked for it, and which ICP plays it supports.

Buyer reality
07 / 11

Bigger buyers require more diligence before they move.

When deals reopened, the conversation often shifted from “do you have the feature?” to “is this worth changing for now?” That is exactly the kind of buyer discipline the $1,000 minimum was meant to force.

Timing and commitment

Some buyers had interest but were not ready to move. The play needs a stronger reason to act now.

Fit and adoption

Some buyers needed more confidence that the product fit their operating model and could be adopted cleanly.

Economics

A higher-value buyer will accept a higher minimum only when the business case is obvious.

Switching risk

For larger prospects, incumbent inertia is real. We need proof that the migration is worth the disruption.

What changes now
08 / 11

Run the roadmap back through the new ICP.

1. Re-test the product
What shipped, what is complete, what still has caveats
2. Match it to buyers
Who asked for it, who values it, who fits the new floor
3. Package the play
Positioning, demo, objections, onboarding story
4. Run and measure
Target accounts, meetings, opportunities, wins, losses

The standard going forward: a release is not commercially ready until we know which buyer it is for, why they should care, and how Sales will turn it into a qualified opportunity.

Board scorecard
09 / 11

The board should see ICP fit, not just roadmap progress.

Product fit

Which higher-value buyer problem does this release solve, and at what workflow depth?

Market package

What is the buyer story, demo, caveat list, and implementation path?

Sales motion

Which accounts are targeted, who owns them, and what next step is required?

Revenue outcome

How many ICP accounts convert to meetings, opportunities, wins, future dates, or clear disqualification?

Immediate next step: use Apollo 3 and Viking 1 as the first test case for this new discipline.
