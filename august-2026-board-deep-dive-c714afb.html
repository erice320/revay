Onboarding challenges
02 / 09

The platform is improving, but onboarding challenges are still centered around specific client workflows

The onboarding leadership review points to recurring blockers around integrations, ticketing/dispatch workflow, billing and payments, product catalog setup, and some expectations. Apollo 3 and Viking 1 did make progress in several areas but have not completely cleared the hurdles.

Improved, not solved

Integrations and migration drag

Still the largest onboarding blocker pattern. Viking 1 created partial coverage around workflow and purchasing adjacency, but QuickBooks Desktop, exact QBO cost push behavior, and third-party integration fit still need account-level validation.

Strongest release coverage

Ticketing, dispatch, and workflow gaps

Viking 1 appears most helpful here: ticketing, dispatch-adjacent workflow, purchasing, invoice history, agreements, and reporting all map to recurring onboarding pain. This is the clearest positive product story.

Trust risk

Billing, tax, and payments exactness

Invoice fields, payment application, prior-payment balances, and tax behavior are not ordinary onboarding friction. If unresolved after graduation, they become cash-flow and client-trust issues.

Process + product

Catalog, project costing, and expectations

Bulk catalog replacement, package/project costing, agreement setup, and implementation readiness are partly product issues and partly expectation-setting issues.

04 / 09

The onboarding read reinforces the same answer as the lost-deal analysis

The historical lost-deal work and the onboarding leadership review point to the same management issue: shipped releases are necessary, but the business only gets credit when Product, Onboarding, Sales, and Marketing can prove the specific workflow, set expectations correctly, and convert that proof into controlled pipeline or successful go-live.

Aligned evidence

Roadmap addressed real pain

Lost prospects and onboarding clients both cite real operational pain: integrations, dispatch/workflow, billing/accounting exactness, reporting, catalog/setup, and implementation risk.

Execution gap

Release-to-revenue is still incomplete

The post-launch lost-deal analysis found activity, but not enough controlled pipeline conversion. The onboarding analysis shows why: buyers and clients care about exact workflow proof, not feature adjacency.

Risk to manage

Do not let “partial” become “positioned as solved”

Over-claiming release impact creates implementation risk and weakens trust when edge workflows are still unsupported or unclear.

Next operating move

Turn blockers into proof assets

Validate the top onboarding scenarios, publish clear “what is solved / not solved” guidance, and feed that into onboarding readiness, sales qualification, enablement, and marketing content.

Onboarding recommendations
05 / 09

Onboarding recommendations: validate the workflows that still create revenue risk

The recommendation is not to treat Viking 1 as a pass/fail release. It is to convert the leadership priorities into a controlled validation and enablement program focused on the named systems, client workflows, and trust risks that still block onboarding confidence.

Priority 1
700

Prove the remaining integration/migration gaps by named system

Kaseya, QuickBooks Desktop, Crexendo, metered printing, Wesco, and QBO cost/duplication workflows need explicit status: supported, partially supported, unsupported, or workaround/process.

Action: create an integration-gap decision matrix and publish sales/onboarding guidance for each named workflow.

Priority 2
665

Separate solved ticketing workflows from feature-adjacent ones

Viking 1 has the strongest coverage here, but ticket-level markup, multiple boards, ticket subtype fields, and security-integrator workflow variants still need proof.

Action: run client-scenario validation and turn confirmed wins into onboarding proof assets.

Priority 3
630

Elevate invoice/payment trust above normal onboarding friction

Billing improved, but invoice fields, tax behavior, bulk payments, payment-application visibility, and prior-payment invoice accuracy remain cash-flow trust risks.

Action: test the exact invoice/payment scenarios with affected accounts before positioning them as solved.

Priority 4
483

Close the catalog, quoting, and job-costing fit gaps

Bulk catalog replacement, package costing through project phases, and inventory-to-project workflows are high-friction implementation moments.

Action: define which gaps are product roadmap items versus implementation playbook gaps.

Visibility

Validate whether new reporting surfaces actually improve daily operating confidence for onboarding clients.

Enablement

Treat access, login, and Rev.io University friction as process/enablement unless tied to specific UX work.

Expectation-setting

Tighten sales-to-onboarding handoff language around unsupported integrations and niche vertical requirements.
