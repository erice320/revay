Opening The Platform Shift Framing
"Cloud changed where the work happened. Agentic AI changes who does the work. And MSPs are uniquely positioned to own that layer."
The Moment We're In
  • Biggest platform transition MSPs have faced since the cloud.
  • MSPs who figure this out in the next 18 months will look like the ones who jumped on cloud managed services in 2012.
  • The ones who wait will be doing break-fix on AI someone else deployed.
Set the Definition
  • Traditional AI: answers questions, assists humans.
  • Agentic AI: takes actions, runs workflows end-to-end, makes decisions with minimal human input.
  • What your customers care about: agents that handle AP processing, follow-up, scheduling, ticket routing — not suggesting what to do, actually doing it.

Card 2 Why MSPs Are the Natural Owner of the Agentic Layer
"You hired us to manage your technology. Agents are technology that runs your business. That's still us."
  • You already own the infrastructure — the data, integrations, identity systems that agents need to operate. That's the foundation.
  • You already have privileged access — customers already trust MSPs with their systems. Agents need exactly that same access. Who better to manage and govern it?
  • Cross-customer pattern recognition — you see what works across 50 clients. An in-house IT person can't replicate that. Your patterns become your IP.
  • The "who do I call when it breaks?" question — has a clear answer if the MSP is managing it. Has no answer if the customer bought it from a SaaS vendor alone.
  • Security and governance is your moat — most AI vendors aren't thinking about this at the SMB level. Monitoring, anomaly alerts, approval thresholds — this is your territory.
Card 3 How to Get Started — The 4-Step On-Ramp
Avoid "boil the ocean." The MSPs gaining traction right now start narrow.
  • Step 1 — Deploy internally first. Don't sell it before you've lived it. Ticket triage, quote generation, onboarding automation inside your own MSP. Build the muscle before you bring it to customers.
  • Step 2 — Pick 3–5 clients to start. Look for: repetitive high-volume workflows, clean-ish data, a pain point they've already complained about. Not your most complex client — your mid-market with straightforward ops.
  • Step 3 — Augment first, automate later. The first agent handles the repetitive 80% so the person can focus on the 20% that needs judgment. Easier sell, faster win, lower risk.
  • Step 4 — Document everything. What worked, what broke, how you priced it, what the customer needed to do. That playbook is your IP and your next sales tool.
Card 4 What MSPs Need to Be Thinking About
  • Data readiness is the hidden gate. Agents are only as good as the data they touch. Most SMBs have messy CRMs and disconnected systems. MSPs who add "AI readiness assessment" to their offering are solving a real problem before it becomes a deployment failure.
  • Security & governance is your moat. An agent with write access is a real risk surface. Who monitors what it did? Who approves actions above a threshold? Who gets alerted on anomalous behavior? Define this upfront — most vendors aren't.
  • Integration complexity is the real work. The demos look easy. Production hits legacy systems, APIs not designed for agents, auth flows that break. Depth in integration = competitive defensibility.
  • Have the liability conversation early. When an agent makes a mistake — and it will — who's responsible? Define human-in-the-loop checkpoints. Never let a customer believe something is fully autonomous when it still needs oversight. That's a trust-killer.
Card 5 How to Price & Sell It — and the Revenue Strategy Behind It
"We're not selling you AI. We're selling you the business outcome — and we're the ones accountable for making it work."
3-Tier Pricing Model
  • Agent Setup / Deployment — One-time project fee. Assessment, design, integration, testing, training. Earn on the intellectual work.
  • Agent Management Retainer — Monthly recurring. Monitoring, tuning, escalation handling, model updates. This is your MRR layer.
  • Outcomes-Based Add-on — Share a % of documented savings. Higher risk, much higher margin, extremely sticky.
The Sales Conversation
  • Don't lead with technology — lead with the cost of the status quo.
  • "Your AP team spends 40 hours a month on invoice matching. What's that costing you? What would you do with those 40 hours?"
  • The ROI case almost always closes itself. Your job is to surface the number.
Revenue Streams This Unlocks
  • AI readiness assessments — sell the evaluation before the deployment
  • Agent deployment projects — one-time, high-margin
  • Ongoing agent management retainers — recurring, sticky MRR
  • Vertical specialization — agents for dental, legal, logistics command a premium
  • Training & change management — often the hardest part, and highly billable
Retention & Defensibility
  • Agents running on a customer's systems deepen the integration moat — switching MSPs means ripping out something running core workflows. Churn drops significantly.
  • Managed intelligence saving $200K/year is not commoditized. Managed IT is.
  • Track record of successful deployments = real IP. The window to build it before the market crowds: 12–24 months.
Card 6 How MSPs Stay on Top of a Fast-Moving Space
  • Build a standing learning practice. The model landscape changes quarterly. Someone on your team — even part-time — whose job includes following this space, testing tools, and bringing knowledge back to the team.
  • Get into vendor partner programs now. AI platform vendors need MSP distribution channels. Early partners get pricing advantages, training access, and co-sell support that late movers won't get.
  • Your peer network is underutilized. The MSPs figuring this out are talking to each other — at IT Nation, in Slack communities, in Reddit threads. If you're not in those conversations, you're missing real-world signal that doesn't make it into vendor marketing.
  • Avoid the shiny object trap. Pick a small stack, go deep on it, build expertise. Breadth without depth doesn't close deals — and your customers will see through it.
Card 7 What Happens to the People? — Human Capital in the Shift
This WILL come up. Have a real answer ready — not a platitude.
"The goal isn't to replace your team. It's to stop paying them to do things a machine does better — so they can do the things only a human can."
The Honest Answer for MSPs
  • Agents are best at high-volume, repetitive, rule-based work. Humans are best at judgment, relationships, escalations, and situations that don't fit the pattern.
  • The first deployment rarely eliminates a role — it changes what that role does. A billing coordinator stops matching invoices and starts handling exceptions, disputes, and process improvement.
  • Where headcount does shrink: it usually comes through attrition, not cuts. MSPs grow revenue per employee rather than laying people off.
The Bigger Opportunity for Customers
  • Most SMBs are short-staffed. They're not replacing people — they're finally covering roles they couldn't hire for.
  • The question shifts from "will I lose my job?" to "what does my job become?" — MSPs who help customers answer that proactively build enormous trust.
For MSPs Internally
  • The technicians who learn to deploy and manage agents become your most valuable people — not your most threatened ones.
  • Reframe it for your team: "We're not automating you out of a job — we're automating the parts you hate, so you can do more of the parts that matter."
  • Training your own staff on agentic tools is the highest-ROI investment you can make right now.
Closing The Call to Action
"The risk of moving too early is small. The risk of moving too late is existential."
  • Your customers will get agentic AI one way or another — through a SaaS vendor, a consultant, a hyperscaler.
  • The question is whether you're the one who deploys it, manages it, and owns that relationship — or someone else is.
  • The conversation to have with yourself as an MSP owner is not "should we do this?" — it's "how fast can we learn enough to start?"
