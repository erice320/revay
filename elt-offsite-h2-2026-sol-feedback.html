01
Executive verdict

The strategic thesis is right; the deck is not yet decision-grade.

Sol agrees the strongest idea is optimizing for durable, implemented, referenceable revenue instead of bookings alone.

What works

  • Cancellation analysis turns implementation pain into strategic evidence.
  • Implementation-readiness gates are directionally right.
  • Certified-claims concept is a strong governance control.
  • The shift from bookings to durable customer outcomes is the right H2 frame.

Why it is not ready

  • It combines scorecard, workshop shell, marketing plan, retrieval log, and brainstorm.
  • It lacks one reconciled financial story, one ICP, one competitive motion, one operating plan, and a short list of decisions.
  • Metric conflicts would undermine confidence in every number.
Recommendation: do not replace the original deck in place. Build a new decision-grade version after locking metrics and choices.

03
H2 math

The H2 requirement is 4.0x to 5.5x H1 pace, not a generic 2x story.

The deck must force an explicit executive choice: hold full-year target, hold H2 budget, or reforecast.

$496KOriginal H2 budget target: about $82.7K/month.
$682KCatch-up needed to hit full-year target: about $113.6K/month.
4.0xMultiple of H1 actual pace required for H2 budget.
5.5xMultiple of H1 actual pace required for full-year catch-up.

Decision options

  • Hold the full-year target and present a credible 5.5x pace plan.
  • Hold the H2 budget and explicitly accept missing the full-year target.
  • Reforecast the year and reset investment, cash, hiring, and operating expectations.

Bridge required

  • Starting recurring revenue.
  • New-logo MRR and expansion MRR.
  • Payments contribution.
  • Contraction, churn, implementation cancellations.
  • Ending recurring revenue.
04
Strategic contradiction

Do not market broad PSA replacement while the internal evidence says replacement is unsafe in key workflows.

Sol recommends separating the H2 default motion from targeted replacement.

Motion A - H2 default

  • Revenue operating layer for complex billing, payments, reconciliation, usage, and multi-service monetization.
  • Coexist with incumbent PSA/RMM/security/accounting when full replacement is unnecessary or not certified.

Motion B - targeted replacement

  • Full PSA replacement only when workflow and migration gates pass.
  • Tigerpaw migrations and select billing-led, field-service, security-integrator, or hybrid MSP/CSP archetypes may fit.

Motion C - aspiration

  • Broad unified-suite competition is a long-term aspiration, not H2 default.
  • Do not make replace-everything claims until workflow parity, migration safety, capacity, and references are proven.
05
ICP choice

The current deck has a market map, not one ICP.

Sol recommends one primary segment, explicit secondary segments, and no-go criteria.

Primary

  • Established hybrid MSP/CSP/security or field-service providers.
  • Complex recurring, usage, device, project, payment, and billing workflows.
  • Lead with revenue operations, billing, payments, and supported integrations.

Secondary

  • Tigerpaw installed-base accounts with validated migration readiness.
  • General MSPs only when acute PSA pain and supported workflow pattern are certified.

Stop / defer

  • Low-complexity MSPs seeking low-cost unified PSA/RMM bundle.
  • Unsupported custom workflows, no data owner, no transition event, or no attach path.

Revenue leakage

The H1 aggregate hides Q2 deterioration and lifecycle leakage.

Sol says the final deck must show where revenue is lost across demand, win, kickoff, live, retained, expanded, and referenceable stages.

Lifecycle loss tree needed

  • Demand -> qualified opportunity -> win -> kickoff -> live -> retained -> expanded -> referenceable.
  • Show conversion, elapsed time, and MRR loss at each stage.
  • Mark unknown joins visibly instead of smoothing over them.

08
Marketing and channel

Convert the marketing activity list into an investment case.

Sol says the marketing plan should be proof-first and tied to expected MRR, not a list of tactics.

Proof-first priority order

  • Customer and migration proof.
  • Competitive-specific proof for the primary ICP.
  • Payments economics and workflow proof.
  • Implementation timeline and readiness proof.
  • High-intent demand capture.
  • Advocacy and reviews.

Every motion needs

  • Target segment and buyer.
  • Offer, budget, owner.
  • Expected leads, qualified opportunities, wins, and MRR.
  • Time to impact, proof asset, kill/continue threshold.

Channel strategy decision

  • Technology alliance-led.
  • Marketplace/distribution-led.
  • Referral/co-sell-led.
  • Direct-led with ecosystem support.
  • Do not call a list of integrations a channel strategy.
