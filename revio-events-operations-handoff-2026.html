01 · Start here

How this role works

Rev.io sponsors roughly 35 trade shows a year across four segments: MSP, Security, Telecom, and A/V. This role owns all of it end to end.

What you own

  • Full logistics per show: contracts, booth ordering, shipping, electrical, lead retrieval, hotels, travel coordination
  • Pre- and post-event communication, internal (team briefs) and external (HubSpot lead emails)
  • Booth material design and ordering, team apparel, swag inventory
  • All sponsorship deliverables per show (logos, ads, passes, listings)
  • iCapture platform administration and lead flow into HubSpot
  • On-site booth setup at select shows

The annual rhythm

  • Contract season (winter): sign next-year agreements, hold booths early for pricing (ISC East / ISC West pattern)
  • Per show, ~8 weeks out: exhibitor kit lands, order services at advance pricing, book travel around published move-in/move-out
  • ~4 weeks out: ship freight to advance warehouse, prep iCapture, send team brief
  • Week of: confirm freight arrival and power drop, run the show
  • Week after: lead cleanup and routing, follow-up emails, team survey, debrief

Support structure

The events lead owns all logistics, including the show-contact outreach: emailing every show for exhibitor kits, deadlines, shipping, electric, and lead retrieval info, tracked in the Notion outreach tracker (outreach email template is on that page). Phoebe Fogerty Bell (Channel intern) assists with specific tasks as assigned and holds admin access to the ASCII Cvent profile and the MSP Summit Informa portal, but logistics is not her role. The tracker workflow itself is how kits and deadlines stop slipping through the cracks; whoever takes this role keeps running it.

02 · Systems

Tools & systems

iCapture by Cvent — lead capture

  • API shows: badge scan works natively. Non-API shows: use the simplified lead form (fewer fields) to cut manual entry
  • ZoomInfo enrichment: capture first name, last name, company; ZoomInfo auto-fills email, phone, title, department. Validated by Usman and Brook at ASCII
  • Capture flow is ordered to feel conversational, not transactional. Keep it that way
  • Before every show: set up the form and QA the full flow before the team is on the floor
  • Known friction: the platform has caused recurring problems. A review was held; there is an internal champion dynamic, so frame future concerns as process improvement, not verdicts

HubSpot — email & RSVPs

  • Pre-event "come see us" emails and post-event thank-you + schedule a demo CTA sequence, templated with bracketed placeholders per show
  • RSVP pulls: filter on recent_conversion_event_name AND first_conversion_event_name (dual filterGroups) to catch all registrants; direct list membership filtering needs a permission we don't have

Notion — operations hub

  • 2026 Rev.io Trade Shows is the hub; each event gets a page from the template (basics, links, expo schedule, staffing, assets, goals, lead capture, map)
  • The hub has an "Event Page Checklist" toggle listing everything a complete event page needs
  • Event Logistics Outreach holds the show-contact database and Phoebe's outreach workflow

Events Hub site — maintenance

  • The Events Hub is the sales-facing source of truth: staff per show (Person filter), booth numbers/sizes, speaking slots, lead and opp goals, 2025 benchmark results
  • Edit Mode on the page itself; changes sync to GitHub repo christinaoudin5/rev-events-hub26 via a fine-grained personal access token (Contents: Read & Write), stored in your browser
  • The live leads dashboard renders client-side; export data directly rather than scraping it

04 · Recurring processes

The processes behind every show

Shipping & freight

  • Freeman is the contractor at ~25 shows (shipping, furnishings, labor), roughly $4,000/show. Booth ships on its own MHA to the Freeman advance warehouse; a separate inventory warehouse holds Rev.io stock
  • Non-negotiable after the Kaseya Connect misroute: confirm carrier, destination, and arrival before every show. The booth once got routed to the inventory warehouse instead of Vegas and never made the show
  • Consolidate freight into one shipment per show (material handling fees apply per shipment)
  • Direct-ship show-specific pieces (backdrop, tablecloth); pull evergreen items from inventory
  • At move-out: box it, label it, or lose it — unlabeled items left in the booth are trash by contractor policy

Electrical ordering — three models

  • Through the GSC's partner (ChannelCon → Encore EventNow)
  • Direct through the venue (Gaylord National, Colorado Convention Center, GWCC). Watch venue-specific advance deadlines and floor plan requirements
  • Included / show-managed (turnkey shows like Navigate; relationship shows like AIN where the show handles power)
  • Always, all models: physically confirm power is dropped at the booth the day before hours start. At XChange Security the drop was missing 2 hours before open and needed an urgent venue escalation

Lead flow, end to end

  • Pre-show: configure iCapture form, QA the full capture flow, purchase the show's scanner/licenses (Cvent, MCI, Whova, varies by show), confirm license emails arrived
  • At the booth: badge scan at API shows; simplified form + ZoomInfo enrichment at non-API shows
  • Post-show: clean the export (title-case names, dedupe, split internal vs external), route to HubSpot, launch thank-you + demo-CTA sequence, then the follow-up nudge
  • events@rev.io is copied on all pre/post event Calendly bookings — keep it on shared calendars for team visibility

Comms cadence per show

  • Team brief (pre-show): logistics, hotel, booth duties/schedule, lead capture instructions, goals
  • External pre-show: HubSpot "come find us" email to leads/attendee lists
  • External post-show: thank-you + schedule-a-demo sequence (2 emails), from templates with bracketed placeholders
  • Internal post-show: team survey + debrief; log results against lead/opp goals in the Events Hub
  • Early and loud: communicate move-in/move-out staffing assignments as soon as dates publish; late assignment comms has burned the team before

Apparel, swag & booth kit

Collect sizes early, order per show, track delivery to the show or to inventory. Standard booth kit travels with every show: banner stand backdrop, tablecloth + runner, retractable banners, swag, MSP/Security sell sheets, iPad holder (Bouncepad), and the supplies kit (tape, scissors, zip ties, Sharpies, extension cord, Velcro dots). Confirm the final asset list per show before shipping.

05 · Playbook

How Christina does things

The judgment layer. These are the patterns that made the program work; steal them.

Escalations that get answered

  • Lead with facts and dollars, not emotion: "$45K to exhibit, ~$100K annual spend across 25 shows" reframed a lost-booth claim from a freight ticket to an account-level conversation
  • Separate the formal claim (team works it) from the relationship conversation (you own it)
  • Ask for the right person, not just a fix: "I'd like to set up a conversation with the right person on your side"
  • For urgent on-site issues, short and specific beats a template: booth #, what's missing, when hours start

Vendor & sponsor negotiation style

  • Warm and conversational, centered on the other side's goals. Never "ROI," "investment," or "make this a yes" in sponsor-facing copy
  • Don't hand a counterparty a cheaper exit: decide which package you're pitching before you email
  • Tiers that include speaking slots are non-discountable; sweeten with add-ons (passes, ads, meal sponsorships) instead of cutting price
  • Hold booths early for next-year pricing before rates move (ISC East / ISC West pattern)

Keep these, don't delegate

  • Sponsor pricing and negotiation
  • Anything going to Evan or other execs
  • Final booth design sign-off
  • Budget approvals

Phoebe (or a successor's support) can prep and draft into all of these; the events lead stays the decision-maker.

Lessons paid for in full

  • Confirm power is physically dropped the day before booth hours (XChange Security, Jul '26)
  • Confirm carrier + destination + arrival for every shipment (Kaseya Connect misroute, Apr '26)
  • Box and label everything at move-out or it's trash (Freeman policy, every show)
  • Chase lead-license emails a week out; whitelist show senders (AIN, Jan '26)
  • Verify hotel outreach is really from the show; scammers target sponsor lists (CANITCON, May '26)
  • Track invoices against signed agreements; signed ≠ paid (ScaleCon INV-0097)
  • Contract fine print matters: "Pax8 Beyond" can't appear in headline positions on non-Pax8 materials (Section 3.1); use "Salt Lake City" instead. Read every co-marketing clause
  • Product name is Revii, not Revy
